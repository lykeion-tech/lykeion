# Checking a claim against given evidence

1. Split the claim into its parts: the entities (method, model, dataset, condition), the measure,
   and the relation asserted (higher, lower, best, equal, a specific value, a difference, a
   trend, a count).
2. Find each entity in the evidence. In a table, match row and column headers carefully:
   abbreviations, the caption, units and footnotes matter. Write down each cell you use as
   (row, column) = value.
3. Do the arithmetic the claim implies (differences, ratios, percentage points vs percent,
   averages, rankings, "all"/"most"). Show it.
4. Decide:
   - `supported` when the evidence shows every part of the claim;
   - `refuted` when the evidence shows any part is wrong (a wrong number, a reversed comparison,
     a wrong entity, an overstatement such as "all" when one is not);
   - `not_enough_info` when the evidence does not contain what is needed to decide (the entity, the
     measure, or the condition is absent), however plausible the claim sounds.
5. Give the passage the verdict rests on, copied word for word, with its source: an attached
   file, a file Experimentation saved, or a source looked up through Lykeion's sources. The
   verdict the hand-in carries is always `supported`, `refuted` or `not_enough_info`; use the
   researcher's label words (for example true / false / unverifiable) only in the answer's prose,
   when they gave them.
Do not bring in outside knowledge to fill a gap in the evidence; that gap is the verdict.
